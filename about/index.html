About me¶

Back to home

Hi, I'm Jen. For about a decade I've been the person teams hand the messy, hard-to-pin-down problems to. I came into it sideways, through language: my first job was localisation testing on AAA console games. From there I grew fast, owning certification for major titles, leading QA teams across languages, cultures, and time zones, and standing up quality from scratch at new studios. Today I'm Technical Operations Manager at a game studio, where I run the entire QA function, release operations, and the internal tooling and automation the team depends on. I've also recently taken over the CI/CD pipelines and DevOps services, learning as I go while keeping operations solid and the team unblocked.

The thread through all of it is the same. I see around corners, I own problems whether or not they're technically mine, and I don't stop until things actually work end to end. Technical enough to dig into the logs, human enough to translate them for everyone else. I like leaving a place more organised than I found it, with the docs written, the process repeatable, and nothing that only runs because someone remembers to ask me.

Where I'm headed¶

Now I'm growing into cybersecurity, DevOps, and AI, and I mean growing into it: hands-on and in the open. That has looked like reviving a self-hosted service that had sat broken for weeks on a shared production server (where a wrong move could have taken the rest of the team's tooling down with it), tracing a silently failing pipeline to a quiet permissions change, building home labs and detection rules, working through TryHackMe, and shipping real things (this site included) while pairing with AI. The portfolio pieces and learning notes here are me showing my work as I go.

How I work¶

I learn by reading the theory and then building and breaking things hands-on. I always try to understand what already exists before I dive in, so I don't end up reinventing the wheel. I write things up, half to cement them for myself and half so the next person has the map I never got. Working with AI comes naturally to me. I like exploring what it can do and pushing it to its limits to learn faster and reach further, using it to open doors into disciplines that would otherwise be out of reach for me right now. I often run a few sessions at once and direct each; I lean on AI where it accelerates me, and I know its limits well enough to catch what's wrong. And I'm always honest about what I actually know versus what I'm still working out, and about where AI helped and where the thinking was mine.

Beyond the terminal¶

Away from the screens (I've got two desktop PCs and a MacBook, so I'm usually glued to at least one of them) I run on synthwave, trance, and preworkout, and I study obsessively, several things at once like my own little college. I train five days a week, mixing lifting, 5k runs, and HYROX-style conditioning, plus a daily 10k steps. It keeps me grounded as much as it keeps me fit, and it's a big part of how I stay steady and feel capable. Quieter evenings are sofa-and-gaming time with my boyfriend, and lately, before those sessions, I sneak in some time to mod 7 Days to Die with Claude, planting little easter eggs for him to discover as we play. And I'm thoroughly owned by a dog named Buggy, who is also a detective (check out his game!).
